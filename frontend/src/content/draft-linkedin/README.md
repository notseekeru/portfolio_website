# LinkedIn drafts

The shape the 2026 studies score best, and why each rule exists. The numbers belong to the studies, so quote them from the source rather than from this page.

Judge every rule here on the within-author lens first, then per follower, then raw. The study that owns these numbers says so itself: where the raw lens and the within-author lens disagree, trust within-author. A rule that only exists on the raw index is not a rule yet.

- [What Works on LinkedIn, 74,119 posts, four lenses](https://www.nbksocial.com/research/what-works-on-linkedin/)
- [postiv.ai, 62,747 posts and 1,340 creators](https://postiv.ai/blog/linkedin-post-study-2026)
- [authoredup, 372,126 posts, engagement by character band](https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-character-limit)
- [linkpost, 359,000 posts, opener types](https://www.linkpost.gg/en/blog/accroche-hook-linkedin)

## Rules

- **One sentence per line, blank line between.** Dense paragraph blocks are the worst-scoring shape and the line-per-sentence look is already the majority habit among the writers measured. Treat it as the floor, not an advantage.
- **Keep the sentences short.** Under 18 words scores 0.97 against 0.87 for longer, and the gap shows up in prose that carries its own argument, so split a 30-word sentence before rewriting it.
- **Open however the idea arrives.** The opener table (a time-anchored story 1.17, a number 1.12, a question 0.88) publishes a raw index and no within-author column, so those ranks are unestablished. What holds on the trusted lens is the archetype: a personal story reads 1.07 within author and 1.16 on comments. The first 200 characters are all most readers see.
- **Length is unresolved, and the correcting lens points down.** Over 2,000 characters leads the raw index at 1.28 but reads 0.88 per follower and 1.02 on comments, against 1.12 and 1.13 for 700 to 2,000 characters. No within-author column is published, so cut repetition and never pad. 3,000 is the cap.
- **Upload the images.** A link card is the weakest format in every dataset that measures it, 0.52 raw and 0.32 per follower against 1.06 and 1.17 for an image. A bare URL costs less near the end, and nothing at all when it stays out of the body.
- **No hashtags.** Per follower the difference is 1.01 against 0.94 with no within-author column, so this is `no gain`, not a cost. One study reports a raw cost, the other finds no effect.

## What is not a format problem

Formatting is saturated, so it cannot differentiate. The content is what carries a post: a number nobody else has, an admission, a diagram that answers a question the reader was about to ask.
